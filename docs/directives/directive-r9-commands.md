# R9-commands: every nav function in one registry, and everything drawn or checked from it

Read first: directive-r9-common.md, docs/editing.md in full (the keyboard standard and its tables), src/kit/text.ts (the `mark(...)` and command rows of its bar, onKeyDown's key handling, `exec`), src/apps/write/editor.ts (the bar: lookItems, alignItems, paperItems, inserts, the KEY_OF map and the alignment keys, `tool(...)`), the bars of the other apps (grep `tool({` and `keys:` across src/apps/*/editor.ts), src/kit/menu.ts (MenuItem, MenuAction, menuKey), src/kit/context.ts (the context bar), src/kit/drawer.ts, src/kit/shell.ts's `renderShortcuts` and the keys dialog, src/kit/app.ts's `Shortcut` and `AppDef.keys`, scripts/make-skill.ts and skill/tinyoffice/SKILL.md (where commands are listed for an agent), test/browser/keys.test.ts or whatever asserts the standard today (grep `docs/editing.md` in test/).

Why: every bar, menu, context bar and shortcut is built inside a render function, and the standard is a Markdown table a person keeps true by hand across thirteen apps. Nobody can answer "is any key bound twice in this scope" or "is every bar button reachable by keyboard" by reading. A registry answers it by graph.

Build:
1. **The registry.** `src/kit/commands.ts`: a `Command` is `{ id, label(), icon?, keys?, scope ('kit' | app id | 'text' for <to-text>), where: ('bar' | 'menu:<name>' | 'context' | 'drawer' | 'keys-only')[], when?(state) => boolean, does(ctx) }`. The kit's commands (undo, redo, find, share, download, print, the marks, the alignments, insert, the size steps, present, …) and each app's are declared once, in a `commands.ts` beside the app's editor, and registered through its AppDef (`commands: Command[]`). Bars, menus and context bars draw from the registry: `tool(cmd)`, `menuOf(cmds)`, the context bar's rows; the keydown handling asks the registry for the command bound to the key in the scopes in force (text inside write inside kit), one place, replacing the per-editor key switches. The old constructions are deleted as each app moves; every app moves in this task (there are thirteen; it is mechanical once the kit's bar and the Word Processor are done).
2. **Derived, never written by hand.** `scripts/commands.ts` writes: the shortcuts dialog's data (the dialog reads the registry at run time; nothing to write), the tables of docs/editing.md between two markers, and the command list in the skill through make-skill.ts. `npm run commands` regenerates; a node test fails when the committed Markdown is stale.
3. **The graph lints** (node tests over the registry, each failing with the command and the rule): no key bound twice within one scope chain (kit ∘ app ∘ text), and a key bound in an inner scope that shadows an outer one is listed in a `shadows` allowlist with a reason; every command in a bar or a menu has `keys` or is reachable by the arrow keys of its bar (the bar is a toolbar: Left/Right walk it; say so once in docs/editing.md); every label is a key of en.ts (through t()); every command's `does` is invoked by at least one test (a coverage map the browser tests write and the node test reads); the Mac and Windows spellings of every key render through one function; no two commands share a label within one scope unless one is `when`-gated and the other not.
4. **The context bar on a phone is a row of the bottom bar, with Cut, Copy and Paste.** Paul, 2026-10-10: "Our menu keeps getting obscured under the native copy paste menu. I feel we should have our own copy paste too on our menu?" iOS draws its selection callout over whatever floats near the words, and no page can stop it over editable text. So on a coarse pointer the context commands (the ones with `where: 'context'`, drawn from the registry) do not float: they take the bottom bar's row while a selection exists (the bar's own row slides back when it collapses, with the chrome's glide), and the row begins with Cut, Copy and Paste as commands of the text scope (copy and cut through the editor's own clipboard path, kit/clipboard.ts, so our fence and our HTML go with them; paste through `navigator.clipboard.read()` where the browser allows and, where it refuses, a one-line hint under the row saying to use the keyboard's paste). On a fine pointer the context bar floats as today, placed from the registry. Read src/kit/context.ts and src/kit/clipboard.ts in full for this; the Word Processor, the Outliner, the Presentation Builder, the Website Builder's words and the Diagram Maker's labels all get it through `<to-text>`.
5. **On a phone the bar appears with the focus and goes with it.** Paul, 2026-10-10: "We could also just hide the secondary bar unless we are editing. It doesn't do anything unless I am, right?" Right for a caret's commands, not for a document's: Look and paper, Present, Play, zoom, the Calendar's views, Show-as. So, on a coarse pointer: the bar is shown while the focus is inside the document (a `<to-text>` with the caret, a selected cell of the Spreadsheet, a canvas being touched or focused in Draw, Diagram and Anim, a chosen row of the Kanban, To-do or Trip, the Calculator always, since its keys are the document) and hidden while nothing in the document has the focus (reading), arriving and leaving with the chrome's glide (R9-chrome's transition, the same one); every command whose `where` holds 'bar' and that needs no selection (`when` true with none: mark them `document: true` in the registry) is also drawn in the drawer's "This document" section under the app's name, so Present, Look, Play and the views are reachable while reading. On a fine pointer the bar is always shown. The rule is one line in docs/editing.md's phone paragraph.
6. **The command palette.** Mod+K (and a button at the bar's end) opens a list of every command in force, filtered as you type, with its keys beside it; Enter runs it; the list is the registry, so it costs the palette's element alone. It is a `<to-menu filter>` if that fits, else a small element of its own.
7. docs/editing.md gains a paragraph saying the registry is the standard's source and the tables are generated; the skill says the same.

Tests: the lints of point 3 as node tests; browser: on a coarse pointer a selection in the Word Processor puts Cut, Copy, Paste and the marks in the bottom bar's row and nothing floats over the words, and the row goes when the selection collapses; Copy puts our HTML and text on the clipboard; on a fine pointer the context bar floats as before; on a coarse pointer the Word Processor shows no bar until the words are tapped and hides it when the focus leaves, the Spreadsheet shows it with a cell selected, the Calculator always, and Present and Look are rows of the drawer's document section; the Word Processor's bar, the deck's, the Outliner's and the Drawing Tool's drawn from the registry with the same buttons and keys as before (the existing bar tests pass unchanged); every key in docs/editing.md's tables does what its row says in at least one app (generated from the tables); the palette opens, filters, runs; the chunk sizes before and after in the report.
