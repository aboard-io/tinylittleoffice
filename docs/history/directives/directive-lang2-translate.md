# Languages, round two: one language file, for New York

Everything in directive-lang-translate.md holds (read it in full first: the file's form, the voice, what stays untranslated, the length rule, the checks). Then this:

**The reader is a New Yorker.** Paul, 2026-10-11: *"make sure the major languages of NYC are addressed and colloquially appropriate for the communities here."* Translate as the community here speaks and writes, not as a ministry or a textbook does:

- **Russian**: the everyday Russian of Brighton Beach and the Russian-speaking city, не канцелярит; ты is wrong for software here, вы without a capital.
- **Ukrainian**: standard Ukrainian as written today, never a transliteration of the Russian file; check every term against the Russian so the two are not the same words in two alphabets where Ukrainian has its own.
- **Polish**: Greenpoint's Polish; the plain second person as consumer software uses it.
- **Bengali**: the Bengali of the city's Bangladeshi communities (Jackson Heights, Kensington): standard Bengali in the Bangladesh usage where it differs, the আপনি register.
- **Hindi**: everyday Hindi in Devanagari as spoken in Jackson Heights and Richmond Hill, with the English loanwords people actually use for computing (फ़ाइल, लिंक, डाउनलोड) rather than Sanskritic coinages; the आप register.
- **Urdu**: everyday Urdu as spoken in Jackson Heights, Brooklyn's Little Pakistan and the Bronx; the آپ register; computing words as people say them (فائل، لنک، ڈاؤن لوڈ); the file is right-to-left.
- **Haitian Creole**: Kreyòl in the official orthography, as spoken in Flatbush and Canarsie, never French dressed as Creole; short sentences.
- **Korean**: the Korean of Flushing and the city's Korean-American communities; 해요체, as consumer software addresses people; the English loanwords Koreans use for computing.
- **Traditional Chinese**: its own translation for the city's Cantonese- and Taiwanese-speaking communities (Flushing, Sunset Park, Manhattan's Chinatown), not a conversion of the Simplified file: the terms Hong Kong and Taiwan software use (檔案, 連結, 下載), written Standard Chinese in traditional characters.

In the report's `glossary`, say the ten terms you decided and why they are the community's words; in `ambiguous`, anything where the city's usage and the standard disagree and what you chose.
