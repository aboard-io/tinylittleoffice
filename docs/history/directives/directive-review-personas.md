# The eight

## low-vision — a low-vision person on a phone

They use the site at 200% and more, with iOS's Larger Text, often in the dark theme, sometimes with the system's zoom on top. What they meet: the size of everything (`src/kit/zoom.ts`, the pinch of `src/kit/pinch.ts`: everything reflows in a text app, a canvas zooms in place), the nav capped at 130% while the words go on, the bar at the foot on a phone, the hamburger in the corner, the reader's pages and their pinch, the templates dialog, the drawer, contrast of every token in both themes (`src/styles/tokens.css`; measure the ratios of text on background, placeholder, hint, link, disabled, the selection bubble, the bar's buttons, the toast, the QR), focus visibility, truncation and overflow at 200% on 390px (the label-widths test measures at 100%; try 200%), the palette, the share dialog, the privacy dialog, the deck's show. Use Playwright at 390×844 with `setSize` or the pinch to 200%, both themes.

## blind — a blind person on a phone with VoiceOver

They navigate by rotor and swipe: headings, landmarks, buttons, form fields; they type into the words editor with the on-screen keyboard and dictation. What you check, by reading the markup the kit draws (`src/kit/*.ts` html templates) and by `page.accessibility.snapshot()` in Playwright on each screen: names and roles of every control (the bar's buttons, the drawer's rows, the templates listbox, the menus, the palette, the dialogs, the link guard, the toast's live region, the deck's show with its sr-only slide announcement), the words editor as a contenteditable (its label, how a heading or a list is announced, how a link is, how a footnote mark is, how the selection bubble is reached), focus management (round nine's walks hold: after Escape, after a dialog, after a command: `test/browser/walks.test.ts`), the reader's pages as SVG (`role="img"` with an alt: what does the alt say for a slide, a table, a diagram; is the document's text reachable at all in the reader, or only as a picture), the home page's structure, the language picker, right-to-left for Arabic and Urdu. Existing a11y tests: find them (`grep -rl aria test/`) and say what they do not cover.

## privacy — a privacy expert, in the manner of Bruce Schneier

Read `docs/threat-model.md`, `docs/crypto/*.md`, `docs/network.md`, `docs/transparency.md`, then the code that makes the claims true: `src/kit/crypt.ts`, `src/kit/privacy.ts`, `src/kit/short.ts`, `src/kit/link-guard.ts`, `src/kit/pictures.ts` and `src/kit/media.ts`, `tinier/tinier.ts`, `media/media.ts`, `public/_headers`, `src/kit/store.ts`, `src/kit/library.ts`, and the tests that hold them (`test/csp.test.ts`, `test/tinier.test.ts`, `test/media-worker.test.ts`, `test/crypt*`). Check each claim against the code; look for what the model leaves out (the browser's history holds the document; a fragment in a bookmark, a sync, a crash report; the title in the head; the app in the path; the QR; the owner link's `o` in the history; what the short-link page's JavaScript could do if the service were malicious; the proxy's cache key; the User-Agent now sent; the 40-bit fingerprint; the `created` time in every head; what `by` claims; signing without verification UI; the languages' safety strings; what a hostile document can do to a reader (links, embeds, pictures, the Website Builder's frame, the sandbox)). Rate each: a real hole, a gap in the model, a wording that overclaims. Say what you would tell a journalist who asked "is it private".

## apple — a software product person at Apple

You have shipped consumer software on iPhone. Use the site on `devices['iPhone 14']` in WebKit for an hour as a new person: the home page, making a letter, a deck, a sheet, sharing, the drawer, the keyboard coming and going, the bar, the hamburger, pinch, dark mode, the reader a link lands on, presenting, the templates. Judge coherence (one way to do each thing?), the first minute (what does the home page tell you to do), naming (every app's name, every menu's words: `src/i18n/en.ts` is the whole vocabulary), consistency of the chrome across apps, iOS conventions kept or broken (the Share sheet, Dynamic Type, safe areas, the home indicator, the keyboard's accessory, scroll behaviour, gestures), performance felt (the chunks: `npm run build` sizes in `budgets.json`; first paint; the lazy app chunks), delight and polish, and what is missing that a person expects (undo across apps, search in a document, a way to find documents, multiple documents, images from the camera, collaboration's absence explained). Twelve findings, ranked as you would for a release meeting.

## mutual-aid — a mutual aid group

A neighbourhood group in the Bronx or Flatbush: twenty volunteers, three languages (Spanish, Haitian Creole, Bengali), old Android phones and one laptop, WhatsApp as the office. They need: a sign-up sheet volunteers fill in, a flyer to print and post, a weekly schedule, a contact list that must not leak, a budget that adds up, a page of resources in three languages, minutes of a meeting, a petition. Try each with the apps and templates as they are (`src/apps/*/templates.ts`, the Spreadsheet, the deck's poster format, the Calendar, the Website Builder's quick links). Judge: can a volunteer do it on a phone in the language they read; does sharing by WhatsApp work (the link length: a sheet of twenty rows is how long; a short link needs the service, which expires; a QR); can several people add to one sheet (no: say what the product offers instead and whether it is enough); printing; what happens on a slow or old phone (the main chunk's size, the lazy chunks, no offline copy since round eleven: what does that cost them on the subway); the privacy of a contact list in a link. Findings as the group would state them, with the code's answer beside each.

## refugee — a scared refugee

They have a phone, a borrowed connection, and documents they cannot afford to lose or to have seen: a letter to a lawyer, a list of names, a timeline of what happened. They read Arabic, Urdu, Ukrainian, Haitian Creole or Bengali better than English. What they need to be true, and to see is true: nothing is kept where they do not want it; a document can be sealed; a link can be shared with one person and die; the site does not phone anyone; they can get out fast; words they understand. Check the Private/Saved switch's words and defaults in their languages, the password dialog (what it says, what a weak password means, that nobody can reset it), the short link's expiry choices, the privacy dialog, the home page's first impression (does it look like something to trust?), the link guard, the About page, the threat model's honesty about the browser's history and about screenshots, the error states (a link that fails, a lost password), the QR, the "offered" reader view (does it leak the document further?), and what a hostile link sent to them could do. Say what would make this person trust it, and what would make them run.

## teacher — a middle school teacher

Thirty students, a class set of Chromebooks and the students' own phones, no accounts allowed by the district, a projector. Needs: a lesson deck (present it; the students get the link), a worksheet to print and one to fill in, a quiz, a syllabus, a seating chart (the Diagram has one), a grade sheet that adds up, an essay plan (the Outliner), a class website, a timeline of a historical period, a field-trip plan (the Trip Planner), a sign-up for parent conferences. Try each with what exists (`src/apps/*/templates.ts`, the home page, the skill's examples). Judge: what a 12-year-old can do without help, what the teacher can set up in ten minutes, what prints well, what the projector shows (the show at 1024×768 and 1920×1080), what is missing for a classroom (a worksheet with blanks? a quiz with answers hidden? a way to collect thirty links?), content safety (the link guard; embeds; what a student could put on a shared page), and reading level of the interface's words (`src/i18n/en.ts`).

## agent — a frontier LLM agent

You are the user of `skill/tinyoffice/SKILL.md`, `public/llms.txt`, `src/api.ts` (`https://tinylittleoffice.com/api.js`), `packages/doc/tinyoffice.ts` (`link`, `read`, `file`, `diff`, `lint`, `measure`). Do the work an agent is asked to do, against the dev site: make a deck with a diagram and a chart, a letter, a sheet with formulas, a trip, a website; lint and measure each; make a short link (the local shortener: `http://127.0.0.1:8787/api`, as the skill's section says); read a document back; diff two versions. Report every friction as a finding: a rule you had to learn by breaking it, a message that did not say what to do, a thing the skill says that is not so, a measure you wanted and did not have, a format that bit you (commas in a Venn, brackets in an address, the two-space rule, a title slide's children), what the lint still cannot say. Round twelve's directive (`docs/directives/directive-r12-agents.md`) was the last pass at this: say what it missed.
