# R5-H: on desktop the document has the window, not a column

Read first: directive-r4-common.md, the merged shell work (git show 4558615 --stat; src/styles/kit.css `.to-main` with `--to-max-w` 1080px, `--to-bleed-l/r`, the stuck bar's margin-left and `::before` strip; src/styles/responsive.css), src/kit/shell.ts, every src/apps/*/style.css, src/kit/home.ts (the home page keeps its column).

Paul (2026-10-10): "Unless documents are hard constrained in their width and height by their form, like decks, we shouldn't really have viewports—take over the whole screen with no border. At least on mobile. On desktop just keep text within margins." Fable's review of the first build at 1280px: the editor still sits in a 1080px column, so the Word Processor's paper is a white block with the page's grey either side (a viewport by another name), and a Kanban Board with five stages is cut off at the column's right edge.

Build: in an editor (not the home page, not dialogs, not the drawer, not the display and compare pages) `.to-main` and the app's bar span the window, with the 16px gutter (and the safe-area insets) as the only inset; the stuck bar still makes room for the menu button. Each app then sets its own measure where it is words: the Word Processor's paper colour runs the full window width (under the bar, edge to edge) and its words stay at the page's measure, centred; the Outliner, To-Do List and Calculator keep a centred measure (their current max-widths) with no frame; the Spreadsheet, Kanban Board, Calendar, Trip Planner, Website Builder, Drawing Tool, Diagram Maker, Animator and Presentation Builder use the full width (the slide and the stage keep their frame and their own max size, centred). The sheet's grid scroller, the board's stages scroller and the canvases size against the window. Dark mode checked. Nothing changes on a phone (already edge to edge). Screenshots at 1280 and 1920 of the Word Processor (thesis template), Kanban Board (studio), Spreadsheet (mortgage), Diagram Maker (The Brontës), Presentation Builder, scrolled and not, in the report's `done`.
