# The review of 2026-10-11: eight people look at Tiny Little Office

Eight reviewers looked at the product: a low-vision reader on an iPhone, a blind VoiceOver user, a privacy expert in the manner of Bruce Schneier, an Apple product person, a mutual aid group in the Bronx, a frightened refugee on a borrowed phone, a middle school teacher, and a frontier LLM agent. They agreed on the architecture and disagreed with some of its edges. All eight would keep the core: the document lives in its link, there are no accounts and nothing is stored on a server, the crypto uses WebCrypto and has test vectors, the network list generates the CSP, and the deck and diagram lints tell an agent the truth. They also agreed on three things that let people down. First, the words the product uses about privacy promise more than the code delivers. "No cookies, no trace" is false while the browser's history holds every document, and a password-protected document gives itself away through Share…, the tab title and the library. Second, people who need the product to adapt to them meet a product that forgets them. The size of everything resets on every link, words split letter by letter at 200%, toasts said inside a dialog are never spoken, and a Display Link is a picture with no structure. Third, the phone basics that iPhone users reach for first are missing or broken: Undo is never on screen, and the presenting controls are white on white in the light theme. Most of the fixes are small changes to modules we already have, and none of them needs a new dependency.

One reviewer's evidence did not hold up when checked against the tree. The privacy expert said the picture whitelist trusts `*.githubusercontent.com` (camo). `src/kit/network.ts` actually lists only `raw.githubusercontent.com` and `user-images.githubusercontent.com`. The redirect-following and `*.archive.org` parts of that finding still stand.

### The themes, ranked

Each theme is ranked by how many people it hurts and how badly.

**1. A password-protected document gives itself away.** Three reviewers found this: the mutual aid group (blocker), the refugee (blocker) and the privacy expert. On a phone, Share… calls `shareNative(this.heading, this.link, await picture(...def.view(this.doc)))` (`src/kit/share-dialog.ts:249`) whether or not the document is sealed. WhatsApp therefore receives a readable PNG of the contact list and its title next to a link that is only ciphertext. "Post the link" puts the title in the X, Bluesky and Mastodon text and in the email subject. `docTitle` (`src/kit/shell.ts:546`) hides the title only while `this.sealed` is pending. Once the document is unlocked, the real title goes into `document.title` and from there into the history, tab sync and the tab switcher. `keepNow()` (shell.ts:525) stores `def.title(this.doc)` with no check of `this.locker`. The refugee measured it: Mod+S rewrote a sealed entry's empty title. Share's `keepLink({ title: this.heading })` does the same. A related leak was found by the teacher: the deck's closing "Take this deck home" QR (`src/apps/deck/view.ts` closingPage/deckLink) encodes the editable deck with its speaker notes, and the Lesson and Trivia templates keep the answers in those notes.

**2. "No trace" is untrue, in eighteen languages.** Four reviewers found this: the refugee (blocker), the privacy expert, the teacher and the mutual aid group. The switch reads "Nothing is saved on this device: no cookies, no trace, only the language you chose" (`src/i18n/en.ts:58` and every translation), and the About text says "nothing is kept". The threat model says the opposite: the address bar holds the document, so the browser's history and its sync hold it too. On a district Chromebook, that history syncs to the district's account. "Forget everything" leaves `tinyoffice.lang`, because `store.ts`'s `ours()` excludes it. The refugee's point is that a chosen language of Ukrainian or Urdu is itself a trace. The Forget-short-link confirm says "Anyone who has it will find nothing there", but anyone who already opened it holds the long link. The "What this protects against" link in About (`drawer.ts:58`) points at a private GitHub repository and returns 404. "Keep on this device and go" turns on Saved for every document without saying so. The privacy expert adds that the "Show pictures from the web" card does not say that showing them tells the picture's site the document was opened.

**3. The size of everything is forgotten, and at 200% words split and controls leave the screen.** The low-vision reader found two blockers and four majors here, and the Apple reviewer and the teacher found parts of the same theme. `zoom.ts` keeps `tinyoffice.size` through `store`, which under Private is an in-memory Map, so every new link or reload is back at 100%. The stepper is about 1,900 px down the drawer and is not a command (no "larger" in the palette, no Ctrl+=). Neither the browser's default font nor iOS Larger Text is read: `html { font-size: calc(16px * var(--to-doc)) }`. At 200% on 390 px, hit areas of 2.75rem leave a to-do's text about 25 px wide, and `overflow-wrap: anywhere` then breaks "Word Processor" into "Wor / d / Proc / ess / or". The calendar's weekday names overlap. The show's Previous button starts off-screen. While typing, the menu button sits at 55% opacity with no edge (1.35:1 over text). The nav stops at 120%, and the browser's own pinch-zoom is switched off. The teacher saw the same shape at 100% on a phone: a Word Processor table cuts "First quarter" to "First…" and breaks "Drawin / g". The mutual aid group saw a 360 px sheet cut every name to two letters.

**4. A screen reader hears nothing at the moments that matter, and a Display Link is a picture.** The blind reader found two blockers. Every toast said while a dialog is open is silent: "Link copied", "Downloaded", "Password copied", "Updated · version n". `<to-toast>`'s role=status sits outside the modal, so the dialog makes it inert. This was verified in Chromium's accessibility tree. A Display Link is flat SVG text, or a single role=img label that hides the content: an outline reads as "Plan", and a deck table reads as its slide title. The export and print pages pass `role="img" aria-label=title` before pageSvg's own pair (`src/kit/exports.ts:114, 135`), so every page is announced as the title. In the words editor, lists are `<li role="none">` with depth only in CSS (`text.ts:1265`). The placeholder is generated content that is read as if it were text. The phone bar, hidden by opacity and transform, stays in the swipe order.

**5. Undo is never visible on a phone.** The Apple reviewer rated this a blocker, and it affects every phone user. `undoTools()` puts Undo and Redo last in a bar that scrolls sideways. Measured at 390 px, Undo's left edge is at 1,411 px in Write and Deck, 660 px in Sheet and 435 px in To-do. While editing, the header steps aside, so nothing visible undoes. Shake-to-undo reaches only `<to-text>`.

**6. Presenting on a phone or a projector.** The Apple reviewer and the low-vision reader found this. In the light theme the show's controls are `#fafafa` icons on white buttons (`deck/style.css:501`), about 1.04:1. Slides cannot be swiped. At 200% the controls' pill is wider than the screen, because `.deck-show .ctl` is not in kit.css's nav list.

**7. Signatures say more than they prove.** The privacy expert and the refugee found this. Any key that signs under the name "Paul" or "UNHCR Legal Aid" gets a green shield and "Signed by …". The fingerprint shows only on hover, and it is 40 bits (`sign.ts:63`, `digest.slice(0, 5)`), which one rented GPU can match in hours. Nothing remembers which key a name used last time.

**8. The gates have holes.** The refugee and the privacy expert found these. The link guard decodes `mailto:` and `tel:` paths with `decodeURIComponent` and draws them with no bidi isolation, so `%E2%80%AE` shows a reversed, fake address in large type. In RTL, the https path's slash jumps to the front. A Website Builder document's links open straight to any address: its page script sets `target=_blank`, and the display iframe allows `allow-popups-to-escape-sandbox` (`shell.ts:687`), so the guard never sees them. The picture proxy decides trust from the first host and then follows redirects to any public host, so an open redirect on a trusted host bypasses "ask first".

**9. Passwords.** The privacy expert and the refugee found this. The dialog accepts "1" without a word, although password.md works out how quickly short passwords fall offline. A sealed link cut short in a chat says "Wrong password", which makes a frightened person think the document is gone. The unsealed path already says "It may have been cut short".

**10. Twenty people cannot hand their copies back.** Four reviewers raised this: the mutual aid group, the teacher, the Apple reviewer and the agent. Nothing tells a recipient that their edits make a new link the sender never sees, and en.ts has no string about copies. Compare treats a table as one block, so one added row reads as "the table was replaced" (`src/kit/diff.ts`). The teacher's Grade book holds ten students, and a row inserted at the class-average row is left out of the average. This is mostly a design question for Paul, with two small fixes inside it: diff by row, and one line in Share.

**11. Every link downloads every app's templates.** The Apple reviewer and the mutual aid group found this. Each `def.ts` statically imports `./templates.ts`, so the apps chunk is 152 KB gzipped, and the first load of /sheet/ or home is 329 KB across 37 files. On 1.6 Mbps at 4× CPU, first paint took 5.3 s. "A definition is light" in apps.ts is no longer true.

**12. Short links at the edges.** Four reviewers found problems here. The teacher found that the platform limiter allows 10 creates a minute per address, and a whole school leaves through one NAT. The refugee found that tinier's page is English only, under the name "tinier", and that no link lasts for one opening. The privacy expert found that the opener, which sees every key, is outside the attested build. The mutual aid group found that flyer QRs encode the long link, and that short links last a week by default and a year at most.

**13. The owner's key stays in the address bar.** The privacy expert found this. `replaceState` writes `?s=…&k=…&o=…` back on every edit while the document is owned, so copying the address from Safari's share sheet hands out edit and delete rights. This is a design question about Private: either a reload loses ownership, or sessionStorage holds it.

**14. Templates are British, on London time, and in English.** The mutual aid group, the teacher and the Apple reviewer found this. Eight of nine calendar templates say `zone: Europe/London`, so the Bronx rota shows the 17:30 shift at 12:30. The templates use postcodes, 07700 numbers, nappies and the Co-op. Translated templates (translation.md §3) are not built. The Trivia template's prize is a bottle of rum, and the Trip Planner offers a pub crawl to a classroom projector.

**15. The agent's checks are quiet outside decks and diagrams.** The LLM agent found this. `LINTS` covers only diagram and deck, plus the sheet's formula errors. A trip drops two stops and a calendar rolls 30 February to 2 March, and both lint clean. Money written as text sums to 0. Excel's habit of counting the header as row 1 multiplies the next row. A chart plots its Total. `measure` returns no values. `link wirte` makes a link. `read` cannot open a short link, and the API has no `shorten`. The skill's `import … from 'https://…/api.js'` fails in Node.

**16. Smaller things, each from one or two reviewers.** Light-theme control edges are 1.48:1 (low vision). Toasts vanish after 2.5 s (low vision). The Theme select is cut at 200% (low vision). Diagrams read as flat buttons, and connections say "a connection" (blind). Sheet cells are named "A1" (blind). The home page has thirteen landmarks and one heading, and key chords appear in names on a phone (blind, Apple). The document has no `lang` or `dir` (blind, mutual aid: a link that suggests Kreyòl). Share is eight stacked sections, with the native Share sheet third (Apple). A Home Screen install under Private loses work (Apple). A document has three names (Apple). The Private/Saved/Keep/Kept naming is inconsistent (Apple). Every document carries its creation minute (privacy). The trip map asks OpenStreetMap before a tap (privacy). Popover is unguarded on old Androids (mutual aid). Opening a sheet selects A1, so the first key replaces it (mutual aid). Timeline era labels are drawn at 8 px (teacher). There is no quiz or hidden answer (teacher). The .md export is TLO verbatim (agent).

### What they were glad of

The low-vision reader praised pinch-to-size with reflow in the text apps, contrast tokens tested in both themes, a dark theme set before first paint, and prefers-contrast, reduced-motion and forced-colors each doing something real. The blind reader found real buttons with aria-pressed, a combobox palette, a calendar that is a real grid with named days, a show that announces each slide, footnotes reachable both ways, and walks that keep focus where it belongs. The privacy expert called the threat model one of the more honest he has read. The CSP, the /network page and the code all come from one list, held by tests. "No logs" is enforced by tests rather than promised. The owner capability is stored as a hash and compared in constant time, and the proxy has sensible SSRF limits. The Apple reviewer praised the templates, the column-in-the-viewport phone design, and the link that "fits in a text message". The mutual aid group had the interface fully in Spanish, Kreyòl and Bengali with no gaps, a 20-row sheet in 870 characters, and no account to make. The refugee logged every request: the site asked the web for nothing until acted on, and a password really does make the link ciphertext. The teacher praised no sign-up, a show that scales cleanly on a projector, and a Display Link that hides the notes. The agent praised a lint that names the slide, quotes the words and gives the fix in 0.3 s.

### Questions for Paul

The reviewers' questions are gathered in forPaul. The ones that block work are these.

- May the size, the theme and (in the opposite direction) the language be treated alike? The question is whether size and theme are kept outside Private and whether the language is forgotten by Forget everything.
- Should the nav grow past 120%?
- Should a Display Link carry a hidden semantic text layer?
- Under Private, should the owner key leave the address bar, at the cost of edit rights on reload?
- Is British English the product's English?
- Is gathering many people's rows in scope before launch?

### Directives drafted

These are in rank order, for round sixteen.

1. `r16-sealed`: a locked document says nothing of itself. This covers Share…, Post, the tab title, the library, keepLink, the QR file name, and the deck's closing QR without notes.
2. `r16-honest`: the privacy words say what is true, in all eighteen languages. This covers no trace, Forget everything, the forget confirm, the picture card, Keep-and-go, and the threat model in the app.
3. `r16-voiceover`: what is said is heard. This covers toasts inside dialogs, list semantics, the placeholder, the hidden bar made inert, the export's double role, and the guard's description.
4. `r16-size`: the size of everything is kept and is easy to reach. This covers keeping it outside Private, size commands and keys, the stepper at the top, the browser's and iPhone's base size, and an opaque menu button when large.
5. `r16-reflow`: nothing splits or leaves the screen at 200%. This covers wrapping rows, break-word, document hit areas, calendar weekdays, the show's controls in the nav and readable in light with swipe, drawer selects, and Word Processor table cells.
6. `r16-undo`: Undo is always on a phone's screen. This covers pinned Undo, no key chords without a keyboard, and Find in the drawer.
7. `r16-signed`: a signature says what it proves, and a password says what it costs. This covers the claim words, remembered keys, a 100-bit fingerprint, the strength hint, and the cut-short message.
8. `r16-gates`: the guard and the proxy hold everywhere. This covers bidi in the guard, the Website Builder's links through the guard, and a trusted picture that does not follow redirects off its host.

## Questions for Paul, in full

Paul, these are the questions from the eight reviewers that change what gets built. Each one is a decision; none is a defect we can just fix.

1. **Size and theme outside Private.** May the size of everything and the theme be kept in localStorage under Private, the way the language already is? r16-size assumes yes and makes it a one-line choice. The low-vision reader called losing the size on every link a blocker.

2. **The language and Forget everything.** Should Forget everything also forget the language? The refugee and the privacy expert point out that a phone set to Ukrainian or Urdu is itself a trace. r16-honest assumes yes.

3. **Bigger controls and browser zoom.** Should the controls keep growing past 120% (NAV_MAX), at least behind a 'Larger controls' switch? Should the words go past 200%? Should Safari's own pinch-zoom come back? Both the touch-action rule and maximum-scale=1 block it now, which leaves a low-vision reader no way past our limits. WCAG 1.4.4 expects controls to scale to 200%.

4. **The translucent menu button.** You asked for it. r16-size makes it opaque only when the size is above 100% or the device asks for more contrast, and gives it an edge otherwise. Is that acceptable?

5. **A Display Link that can be read.** May a Display Link carry a hidden semantic text layer under its pictures, built from each app's HTML writer or a generic one over the kernel? The blind reader called this the biggest gap. Without it, someone sent a link to read hears flat text or a single label. The low-vision reader asks for the same thing as a visible 'Read as text' toggle: reflowed, at the person's size, in the theme's colours rather than white paper. This would be the next round's first directive, and it is L.

6. **The owner's key in the address bar.** Under Private, the owner's key (o, k) stays in the address bar, and copying the address hands out edit and delete rights. There are two ways out. The owner could lose ownership on reload and get a 'Copy owner link' button instead. Or ownership could live in sessionStorage, which means Private writes something for the life of a tab.

7. **The short-link opener.** The opener on tinier sees every short link's key, and it sits outside the attested build. Would you move it into the site? That costs one redirect. Two smaller choices go with it. Should there be a one-opening lifetime? The refugee asked for one; it needs an 'Open' tap so a preview robot doesn't spend it. And should the default lifetime be a day instead of a week? Separately, the shortener's limit of 10 a minute per address fails a classroom behind one school NAT. May it be raised?

8. **Gathering many people's work.** Is several people adding to one document in scope before launch? The mutual aid group (sign-ups), the teacher (thirty hand-ins) and the Apple reviewer (nobody is told their edits make their own copy) all hit this. Options, smallest first:
   - one line in Share: 'Whoever opens it gets their own copy; ask them to send their link back'
   - Compare diffs a table row by row and can 'Take these rows'
   - a sheet url cell that shows a pasted link's title, by and signed state, decoded locally

   If collaboration will never exist, may we say so where people share?

9. **Which English.** Should the English be US English? en.ts uses Colour, postcode and programme while the paper default is Letter, and the templates use 07700 numbers, nappies, the Co-op and London time. A Bronx rota shows the 5:30 pm shift at 12:30. Should templates take the reader's time zone when opened? I would make that change regardless. And should a classroom see templates with a pub crawl or a bottle of rum?

10. **Copy on the home page.** Is 'no copy on the home page' absolute? The Apple reviewer and the refugee each ask for one line of state, such as 'Your document lives in its link' or 'Private: nothing kept here', until the first link is copied.

11. **Home Screen installs.** Should an app added to the Home Screen keep documents by default? Under Private, a relaunched standalone app opens the home page with the document gone, and iOS gives no warning when you leave.

12. **Simplifying Share on iPhone.** On an iPhone, may Share hand the job to the system Share sheet (first, full width) and fold password, short link, QR and embed under 'More'?

13. **The threat model's link.** The threat model link in About is a 404 while the repository is private. May the threat model be published at /threats on the site before launch? r16-honest puts its limits into the About text in every language either way.

14. **Mod+K.** Mod+K is both Link and the palette, which breaks editing.md's rule. Which keeps it?

15. **Hidden answers for teachers.** Is a quiz worth one TLO attribute, `{.answer}`, revealed while presenting or on a tap, plus a 'Quiz with answer key' template that uses the password-locked fence?

16. **Production's api.js.** Production's api.js and llms.txt are the old tree's: v0 links and no measure. Should they be replaced before launch, or should the skill say it describes the dev site?

17. **A language for each document.** May the TLO head carry an optional `lang:`? It would decide which voice reads a document, and it would let a link open in Kreyòl on a phone set to French.

One correction to the reviews: the privacy expert said the picture whitelist trusts `*.githubusercontent.com`, including GitHub's camo proxy. It doesn't. `src/kit/network.ts` lists only the exact hosts `raw.` and `user-images.githubusercontent.com`. The redirect and `*.archive.org` parts of that finding still stand, and r16-gates fixes them.

## Round sixteen

The eight directives drafted from this review are `docs/directives/directive-r16-*.md` with `directive-r16-common.md`, run by `docs/directives/r16.js`. Two amendments to the drafts: the size commands take no key chords (Ctrl+=, Ctrl+- and Ctrl+0 stay the browser's), and a trusted picture's redirect is followed to any trusted host, not only the same host, so archive.org's download addresses keep working.

## The eight reviews

### low-vision: a low-vision person on an iPhone, using the site at 200% and more, often in the dark theme, sometimes with iOS Zoom as well

The text editor reflows well at 200%. Pinching to make everything larger is a good idea, the tokens are measured and pass in both themes, and the dialogs turn into readable full-screen sheets. Outside the editor it falls apart. Every new link or reload goes back to 100%, because Private keeps the size only in memory, and the control to set it again is about 1,900px down a long menu and is not a command. At 200% on a 390px phone, words split letter by letter: the To-do List's task text, the Kanban Board's column names and the home page's app names ("Wor / d / Proc / ess / or"). The calendar's weekday names run into each other, and the presentation's Previous button is pushed off the screen. While I type, the menu button fades to 55% opacity and has no visible edge, and the menu, the bar and the dialogs stop growing at 120%. Because the browser's own pinch-zoom is switched off, I can't get past these limits, and a Display Link gives me 8px words on a bright white page with no way to reflow them.

- **The size of everything is forgotten on every new link and every reload** (blocker, S) — src/kit/store.ts (Private keeps preferences in an in-memory Map); src/kit/zoom.ts KEY 'tinyoffice.size'; src/kit/i18n.ts (language is already exempt)
  Playwright WebKit, iPhone 14, /#/v1/write. Set to 200% from the menu, the root font size is 41.6px. After page.reload() it is 20.8px (100%). Changing the hash in the same page keeps it, but opening a link from Messages, Mail or a QR code is a new page load. Private is the default, so a low-vision person sets the size again on every document. The privacy dialog says to 'Turn on Saved in the menu' to keep things, but that also keeps their documents.
  *Fix:* Treat the size like the language: a preference that leaves no trace of any document. Read and write 'tinyoffice.size' straight to localStorage in both states, as i18n.ts does for 'tinyoffice.lang'. Add it to store.ts's `ours` exemption beside KEEP and lang, so 'Private again' still clears it if Paul wants that. Do the same for 'to-theme', which public/theme.js already reads from localStorage. Test in test/zoom.test.ts: with Private on, keepSize(2), then a fresh module load gives size() === 2. Update docs/threat-model.md to name the one extra key.
- **Words split letter by letter at 200% in the To-do List, the Kanban Board and on the home page** (blocker, M) — src/apps/todo/style.css .todo-row/.todo-tick/.todo-body; src/apps/board/style.css .board-head; src/styles/kit.css .to-app-open .to-app-name (overflow-wrap: anywhere) and --to-touch: 2.75rem in tokens.css
  At 200%, 390×844, light theme. To-do List: the task placeholder 'Task' takes four lines, one letter each. Kanban Board: the column names 'To do' and 'Doing' run down the column one letter per line. Home: 'Word Processor' breaks as 'Wor/d/Proc/ess/or', and 'Outliner' as 'Outl/iner'. Cause: inside the document, hit areas are 2.75rem (114px at 200%), so in a to-do row the grip (1.25rem), tick (114px), star and menu (114px each) take the whole 390px and leave the text column about 25px. With overflow-wrap: anywhere, the browser then breaks inside words rather than wrapping the row. The label-widths test only measures at 100%.
  *Fix:* 1) Let the trailing controls go below when the words have no room: give .todo-row, .board-head and .to-app-head flex-wrap: wrap, and give the words' box flex: 1 1 8em. 2) Use overflow-wrap: break-word instead of anywhere on names and labels (.to-app-name, the board head's to-text), so a word breaks only when it is longer than the whole line. 3) Keep a document row's hit areas at the nav's scale: min(2.75rem, calc(2.75 * var(--to-nav-font))). Test: an e2e at SIZE_MAX (390px, WebKit) for the home page and each app's first template. For every text node in main, each word's Range must give a single client rect, which means no word is split across lines.
- **Calendar weekday names overlap one another at 200%** (major, S) — src/apps/cal/editor.ts:940 (weekday: 'short'); src/apps/cal/style.css .cal-wd (lines 31, 463)
  At 200% on the phone, the header reads 'SUNMONTUEWEDTHUFRI SAT' with the letters printed over each other. Each column is about 55px and 'WED' in uppercase at 1rem×0.6875 is wider than that. The agenda already uses weekday: 'narrow' (editor.ts:1022), but the month grid does not.
  *Fix:* In .cal-row, render both forms, <abbr title=long><span class=short>…</span><span class=narrow>…</span></abbr>, and pick one with a container query on .cal-grid, for example @container (max-width: 34rem) shows the narrow one. Also give .cal-wd overflow: hidden; text-overflow: clip as a backstop. Test: at SIZE_MAX, no two .cal-wd rects overlap.
- **In presentation mode, the Previous button goes off the left edge at 200%** (major, S) — src/apps/deck/style.css .deck-show .ctl; src/styles/kit.css:42 (the nav list)
  Opening a deck with ?show at 200%, the Previous, Next and 'Edit this deck' buttons are each 114px wide. The pill holding them is pinned to the right and wider than the 390px screen, so Previous starts at x<0 and is cut off in the screenshot. A slide with notes adds a fourth button and makes it worse. .deck-show .ctl is not in the nav list, so its buttons use the document's 2.75rem rather than the nav's capped size.
  *Fix:* Add `.deck-show .ctl` to the :is(…) nav list in kit.css, which caps it at 120% like the other controls, and give it max-width: calc(100vw - 1.5rem) with flex-wrap. Test: in e2e/deck-phone.spec.ts at SIZE_MAX, every .ctl button's rect lies within 0..390.
- **The size control is about three screens down the menu, and is not a command** (major, S) — src/kit/drawer.ts:276-279 (stepper at the end of the drawer); src/kit/commands.ts (no size command); palette
  At 100% the drawer scrolls 2,209px and the size stepper starts at 1,891px, after This document, the app's settings, 'Find app, template', all thirteen apps, Open a file, Private and Theme. At 200% it starts at 2,336px. Typing 'larger' or 'size' in the palette finds nothing, because stepSize is called only from drawer.ts. There is no Ctrl+= or Ctrl+- either. The person who needs this control most is the one least able to find it at the bottom of a long list of small words.
  *Fix:* Move the 'Size of everything' stepper into the drawer's head row next to Language and Theme, or make it the first item. Register three commands in kit/commands.ts: 'Everything larger' (Ctrl+=), 'Everything smaller' (Ctrl+-) and 'Normal size' (Ctrl+0), each calling stepSize. Then the palette and the keyboard reach them, and test/commands-graph covers them. Add the strings to en.ts.
- **While typing, the menu button is translucent and has no edge, and it is the only way to the menu** (major, S) — src/styles/responsive.css:195-199 (to-chrome[editing] .to-burger opacity: 0.55)
  Measured from the tokens at opacity 0.55: the icon is 3.95:1 over blank light paper and 4.84:1 over dark paper. When a line of words scrolls under it, which happens at 200% because the words reach the corner, it drops to 1.35:1 in light and 1.25:1 in dark. The button's own surface against the paper is 1.00:1 in light and 1.04:1 in dark, so there is no visible edge. The screenshot at 200% in light shows a pale grey icon floating in white. The size, Share and Theme controls are all behind it.

### Privacy expert, in the manner of Bruce Schneier. I read the threat model, the three crypto documents, the network page and the transparency plan, then checked each claim against the code that is supposed to make it true: crypt.ts, short.ts, privacy.ts, store.ts, i18n.ts, link-guard.ts, pictures.ts, media.ts, tinier/, media/, _headers, shell.ts, sign.ts, privacy-dialog.ts, the Website Builder's view and frame, and the 18 language files. The work was code reading only; I did not drive a browser.

This is one of the more honest privacy designs I have read. The document lives in the fragment, there is no server storage, the crypto is WebCrypto with test vectors, the network list generates the CSP, the no-logging rule is held by tests, and the threat model admits most of what it does not protect against (browser history, D1 Time Travel, trust in whoever serves the code). The holes are in the edges between the model and the shell. The owner's capability key (`o`) stays in the address bar for the whole editing session. A password-protected document's real title goes into the browser's history once it is unlocked. The signature UI shows a green "Signed by Paul" whose name anyone can claim, checked against a 40-bit fingerprint that a GPU can match in hours. The picture whitelist's wildcard and the proxy's redirect-following can get round the "ask before showing pictures" gate. Website Builder documents are not covered by the link guard. If a journalist asked "is it private?", I would say: it is private from the operator's servers as long as you trust the code they serve; it is not private from your own browser, from anyone you give the link to, or from a weak password; and the "no trace" wording on the Private switch should be changed before a frightened person relies on it.

Whoever receives that link can PUT a new version under the public short link everyone else is using, or DELETE it (tinier.ts prove()). docs/crypto/short-links.md mentions only that o 'stays in the browser's history'; it does not cover this copy-paste path. The capability is meant to be the owner's alone, and this hands it out in normal use.

- **The picture whitelist's wildcards and the proxy's redirect-following let a tracking picture show without the reader being asked** (major, M) — src/kit/network.ts (trusted '*.githubusercontent.com', '*.archive.org'); src/kit/media.ts trusted()/trustedHost(); media/media.ts follow() (redirects to any public https host)

- **The 40-bit fingerprint can be forged in hours, not "expensive but possible"** (major, S) — src/kit/sign.ts:61-65 fingerprintOf (5 bytes of SHA-256); docs/threat-model.md 'A determined forger matching a fingerprint'; docs/crypto/signing.md
  Matching a given 40-bit fingerprint is a second-preimage search of about 2^40 ≈ 1.1×10^12 tries.   *Fix:* Show at least 80 bits, better 128: for example four groups of five base32 characters (100 bits), or six words from a list. Keep the hyphenated look. Update signing.md, the threat model and the vectors' fingerprint field, plus a test that fingerprintOf returns the new length.

Glad of: The architecture is right. The document lives in the fragment, there are no accounts and nothing on the server to breach or subpoena; the threat model says so plainly and does not try to reassure. It is honest about uncomfortable things most products leave out: browser history and sync, D1's Time Travel window, trust in whoever serves the code, the app name in the path, ciphertext length. The crypto is WebCrypto with no library, has byte-level documents and test vectors, and a test fails if anything in the site calls the fixed-IV helpers (sealAt, sealShortAt). The CSP's connect/img/frame/media directives are generated from the one list of hosts in src/kit/network.ts, which also generates the public /network page, and tests hold all three to each other; very few products can show that. 'No logs' is enforced (observability off, no console calls, both held by tests) rather than promised. The owner capability is well designed: only its hash is stored, it is compared in constant time, and the service gets o only in the one request that proves it. The link guard shows the punycode hostname and focuses Cancel. Pictures from non-whitelisted hosts wait to be asked for and go through a proxy with sensible SSRF limits (https only, public names, no credentials in the URL, a size cap, a timeout, redirect checks). Sealed documents are kept in the library with no title. The password generator is uniform by rejection sampling. Reproducible builds with attestation are already built for the site. Private by default is the right default for the people this is for.

Questions: 1. To fix the owner key in the address bar under Private, would you accept the owner losing edit rights on reload (and getting a 'Copy owner link' button instead), or should the owner capability live in sessionStorage, which would mean Private writes something for the length of a tab? 2. Should the language really survive 'Forget everything'? For a refugee, the language they chose is itself a trace. 3. Would you move the short-link opener into the site, so the code that sees every short-link key is covered by the attestation? It costs one redirect. 4. A small wording point: short-links.md says a 403 and a 404 'carry the same body' so 'neither tells an outsider which ids exist', but the status codes differ and GET /api/<id> answers that question for anyone. The constant-time comparison protects only the owner hash. Do you want the docs to say that, or the PUT/DELETE answers made identical? 5. Is the signature feature meant to resist a forger, or only accidental change? If only accidental change, the green 'Signed by Paul' should say less; if a forger, the fingerprint needs to be longer and keys need to be remembered.
